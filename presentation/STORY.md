# Aether’s Trial — Windborne

## Story treatment

**A traveler without a past. A valley losing its wind. A reason to keep moving.**

This is the story treatment for an unofficial, Genshin Impact-inspired exploration prototype. Its central premise is now readable in the game’s **The story of Windrise** journal, available from the title and pause menus. The journal describes Aether’s missing memories, the damaged wind-shrine network, and guardians that mistake travelers for threats; Lyra’s introduction and the final victory message reinforce that story. The 30-second illustrated opening shows the cause of his memory loss. The dramatic elaboration below does not imply playable memory sequences, branching conversations, or additional in-engine cutscenes.

## The premise

Aether wakes on the grassy overlook above Windrise Valley. He remembers neither the road that brought him here nor the people he has left behind. A small floating companion finds him after the disaster. She does not know his past or what happened at the shrine; she offers the one certainty he has: they can find a way forward together.

Below them, ordinary life continues. A smith lifts his hammer, a runner delivers bread, and a gardener tends the vineyard. Yet the wind that once linked the valley’s ancient gates has begun to fail. Scattered wind sigils still carry fragments of its power. The old guardians remain awake, but their broken instructions make every approaching traveler look like a threat.

In Mondstadt, Lyra asks Aether to gather three sigils and listen to what remains of the wind. The task begins as a search for answers about himself. It becomes a promise to protect a place that has welcomed him.

**Central question:** Can Aether choose who he will become before he remembers who he was?

## Why Aether lost his memories

An ancient wind shrine breaks, sending uncontrolled energy toward the village. Its guardian turns on him. Aether parries its strike, dashes past it and cuts through its defense, then seals the breach to protect the people below. The seal holds, but it draws on his memories to sustain itself. Images of his past break apart into fragments of light, and the blast carries him to the overlook.

The companion finds him only afterward. The audience sees what Aether can no longer remember; she is not a witness explaining his history. His kindness survives even though his memories do not. The playable journey begins with that choice already made.

This is an original backstory for this fan-made prototype. It is not the official Genshin Impact opening, which concerns the twins’ separation and sealed powers.

## The journey in three acts

### Act I — Follow the breeze

**Setting:** Windrise Overlook → Mondstadt → the valley’s paths, lake, and trees.

Aether’s first view is an invitation: blue water, green hills, distant windmills, and a road leading toward people. The companion encourages him onward. Meeting Lyra gives his wandering a direction. He gathers three wind sigils, learning the landscape and meeting its residents along the way.

**Playable goal:** Speak with Lyra, collect three of the valley’s six sigils, and return to her.

**Emotional turn:** Curiosity becomes responsibility. The traveler begins to care what happens here.

### Act II — The guardian’s mistake

**Setting:** Mondstadt Fields and the roads beyond the village.

The sigils stir the old gates. Lyra explains the path ahead: first the guardian of the fields, then the drake in the northern ruins. Aether encounters creatures with different strengths and learns to watch their warnings instead of answering every threat with the same attack. When the field guardian falls, the northern trial becomes available.

**Playable goal:** Defeat ten regular enemies and the Mitachurl in the first trial.

**Emotional turn:** Strength becomes judgment. Aether’s task is to restore safety, not simply win fights.

### Act III — Where the storm sleeps

**Setting:** Stormterror Ruins.

The ruins hold the final challenge in restoring the valley’s ancient wind network. Aether faces fifteen regular foes and the Storm Drake. The victory text announces that the wind shrines can breathe again and reminds him that the people of Windrise will remember what he chose to protect. In a future visual telling, the wind would return to its familiar course and the valley’s storm would begin to settle; the current game conveys this resolution through text.

Aether does not recover every memory. He gains something he can act on now: a place he has helped, people who know him, and a reason to continue. The ending leaves the world open for exploration rather than closing the road behind him.

**Playable goal:** Clear the second trial; the game confirms the Storm Drake’s defeat, closes the story through its victory message, and returns the player to the valley.

**Emotional turn:** Aether moves from asking “Who was I?” to deciding “Who will I be?”

## The world

Six discoverable landmarks give the compact valley a sense of direction:

- **Windrise Overlook:** the beginning, with a broad view of the journey ahead.
- **Mondstadt:** a welcoming settlement, Lyra’s home, and the center of daily life.
- **Cider Lake:** a quiet contrast to combat, with water, shorelines, and a fisher.
- **Windrise:** the great tree and a place for slower exploration.
- **Dawn Winery:** vineyards, deliveries, and the valley’s working countryside.
- **Stormterror Ruins:** the northern destination and the story’s final trial.

These are simplified fan-made interpretations of familiar Genshin locations inside one local WebGL prototype, not reconstructions of the original game’s world or scale.

## What exists today, and what the treatment adds

**Implemented:** Local browser rendering with WebGL; six landmarks; movement and exploration; nine residents with simple routines and dialogue; Lyra’s sigil quest; twenty-four roaming enemies in eight camps with six archetypes; sword and elemental combat; two sequenced trials; progression, treasure, waypoints, and local saves. **The story of Windrise** journal presents the missing-memory premise, damaged wind network, mistaken guardians, three chapters, and theme. Lyra’s opening conversation and the final victory message now connect those ideas to the playable quest. The 30-second opening illustrates the shrine’s eruption, Aether’s duel with its guardian, his sacrifice and memory loss, and his awakening. It uses synthesized female narration, not a recorded human performance.

**Story treatment / future scope:** Expanding Aether’s missing-memory mystery into playable events; revealing the wind network’s history in detail; scripted companion conversations; collectible or playable memory fragments; additional story cinematics; a visible storm-clearing sequence; and additional character arcs. The short opening illustrates the cause of his missing memories; deeper exploration of the broken network and confused guardians through additional cinematics or gameplay remains future work. Branching dialogue and multiple endings are not implemented.
